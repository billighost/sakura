# Sakura's voice

Updated 8 November 2026 after a review of the product interface and the listener's feedback.

Sakura is a quiet, familiar place for music. Its voice is warm, composed, and clear. The listener chooses what to hear and how long to listen. The interface gives them room to do that.

## The voice in practice

- Keep the established lines **Music, with room to breathe**, **Let the world wait a little**, and **your little music world**. They describe Sakura's setting without asking for attention.
- Put warmth in page introductions, illustrated stories, and a few short details. Give buttons, menus, status messages, and errors direct names.
- Describe music and listening choices. Recommendations use likes, artists, genres, and recorded listening; they do not describe someone's personality or emotional needs.
- Use a single musical or botanical image where it adds atmosphere. Avoid repeating "little", "garden", "home", "world", and "growing" in every sentence.
- Make the next action clear: **Sign in**, **Play top songs**, **Load more songs**, **Export my data**, **Remove this download?**.
- Keep **Find my soundtrack** as the recognizable action for starting a personalized queue. Explain source limits where they matter, such as downloaded music.
- In loading and recovery states, describe what happened and the available action. Avoid turning unavailable songs, transfers, or account errors into stories about finding a home.
- Data controls use precise language. Clearing history, excluding a song, unliking a song, and removing a download have different effects.
- Preserve the more expressive voice of Sakura Bloom's illustrated worlds. Tie the listening story to the recorded songs, artists, and period rather than claiming the music became the listener.

## Examples

| Previous wording | Revised wording |
| --- | --- |
| Come in. Stay for a song. | A quiet place for your music. |
| The songs that feel like you, and the ones that might. | Your favorites, a few new discoveries, and a quiet place to listen. |
| This is what you sound like. | Your music, over time. |
| Stay here a while | Play top songs |
| A little room to fall in love. | No liked songs yet. |
| A garden that belongs to you | Your data, your choice |
| A little space to start again? | Clear selected data? |
| Let this song leave the garden? | Remove this download? |

Home's main heading returns to **Let the world wait a little**. Discover uses **A world of good sound**. Profile uses **Your music, over time**. The artwork and spacing carry the atmosphere; the copy stays easy to understand.

## Research and rationale

The user's direction and Sakura's existing voice are the primary sources. The original Home, authentication, opening animation, and brand details already established a quiet place to listen. This revision restores that tone and removes invitations to stay, assumed emotional connections, and unnecessarily decorative action labels.

[Microsoft's official voice guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human), read on 8 November 2026, describes a warm, relaxed voice with crisp clarity. It recommends fewer words, everyday language, obvious decisions and next steps, and enough information for people to act confidently. Those principles support Sakura's calm setting without copying another product's personality.

The [music discovery research](./PERSONALIZED-FEED-RESEARCH.md) supports concise recommendation explanations and distinct preference, feedback, and deletion controls. Sakura's language describes the actual selection evidence and user action rather than presenting the recommendation system as an emotional authority.

The review covers Home, Discover, Profile, preferences, data controls, authentication, onboarding, search, library, downloads, song options, playback, queue, sharing, lyrics, audio comparison, Bloom, mix descriptions, recommendation reasons, and recovery messages. Catalog titles, artist names, lyrics, route identifiers, and functional behavior are preserved.
